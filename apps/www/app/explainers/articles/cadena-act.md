## What it is

The CADENA Act would require every national government agency to publish its budget records, from the first budget call to the last audit finding, on one public portal that nobody can quietly edit. CADENA stands for Citizen Access and Disclosure of Expenditures for National Accountability; the word also means "chain" in Filipino, a nod to the blockchain the portal is meant to run on.

It began life as Senator Bam Aquino's "Blockchain the Budget" bill and was renamed CADENA in late 2025, as the flood control scandal pushed budget transparency to the top of the national agenda. The idea is simple to state: if every appropriation, release, contract and payment is posted within days, in a format anyone can search and download, then a citizen can follow a peso from the line item that created it to the contractor who received it.

## What "blockchain" means here

A blockchain is a record kept in many copies at once, where every new entry is sealed with a fingerprint of the entry before it. The fingerprint, called a hash, is a short code worked out from the entry's contents: change a single figure and the code comes out different. Because each entry carries the fingerprint of the one before, altering an old entry breaks the link to everything after it, and every copy can see the break.

Nothing is erased. A correction is a new entry that points back to the one it corrects, so the original stays visible beside it.

{{figure:blocks}}

For a budget portal, that brings three things:

- **Records that cannot be quietly changed.** Once a release order or a contract is posted, editing it afterwards shows up as a broken link, not a silent fix.
- **A history anyone can read.** A reader sees what was first posted, what was corrected, and when, because corrections are added rather than written over.
- **Copies outside one office.** Accredited mirrors run by universities, media and civil society can check, entry by entry, that they hold the same record as the government.

What it cannot do is make a record true. A blockchain proves an entry has not changed since it was posted; it says nothing about whether it was right when it was posted. A padded contract posted on time is still padded. That is why the bill pairs the portal with posting deadlines, penalties for false entries, and the Commission on Audit.

## How it got here

The Senate passed it 17 to 0 on 15 December 2025. The House has not passed it ten months later.

So the bill is written, passed by one chamber, endorsed by the President, and stuck in a House committee. That is where things stand today.

## What it would put online

Section 5 of the bill lists the documents, stage by stage, and names the office responsible for each. The accountable officer has seven working days from the document's approval or issuance to post it. Corrections are versioned, so the earlier entry stays visible.

| Stage of the budget | What gets published | Who posts it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation | Budget call, every agency's budget proposal forms, the NEP, the BESF, the President's budget message | DBM, line agencies |
| Legislation | Every version of the appropriations bill, committee reports, each amendment with its proponent named, the bicameral report, the veto message, the GAA | House, Senate, DBM |
| Execution | Agency budget execution documents, SAROs, NCAs, fund release receipts, registries of allotments and disbursements, disbursement vouchers | DBM, Treasury, agencies |
| Procurement | Program of works, bill of quantities, unit price analysis, bid notices, evaluations, abstracts of bids, beneficial ownership disclosures, notices of award, contracts, progress billings, inspection reports, completion certificates | Head of the procuring entity |
| Accountability | Financial statements, COA audit reports and observation memoranda, agency action plans, oversight hearing records | COA, agencies, Congress |

Each record must carry a linkage identifier so that an appropriation can be traced to its final payee. Public expenditure, contract and supplier information is expressly declared not personal data, so the Data Privacy Act cannot be used to delay disclosure.

The penalties have teeth. Missing the seven-day deadline is an administrative offence: reprimand or up to six months' suspension on the first, dismissal and perpetual disqualification on the second. Failing to post for thirty working days without cause, posting false data to fake compliance, or destroying a document that should have been posted is a crime carrying six to fifteen years in prison and a fine of up to three million pesos.

{{figure:deadlines}}

A National Budget Transparency and Accountability Council, co-chaired by the DICT and DBM secretaries, would run the system, with COA, DOJ and DOF as members and four citizen seats for civil society, academe, media and the tech sector. Rollout is phased: agency websites within nine months, the five biggest spenders on the portal within two years, everyone within three.

## Who it covers, and where the Bangsamoro falls

As written, the CADENA Act does not reach the Bangsamoro Government's own spending. Section 4 covers national departments, bureaus, agencies and instrumentalities, GOCCs and their subsidiaries, plus PPP and foreign-assisted projects that pass through national mechanisms. Local government units are expressly excluded; they are only "encouraged to adopt similar mechanisms."

The Bangsamoro Government is named nowhere in the text. It is neither a national agency nor an LGU under the Local Government Code; it is an autonomous regional government with its own parliament, its own appropriations law and its own procurement. That leaves it in a gap the drafters did not address.

In practice, CADENA would show the national side of the Bangsamoro's money and stop at the regional border. A reader could see the DBM's release of the annual block grant and the Special Development Fund, the SAROs and NCAs that carry them, and national agency projects in the region such as DPWH and DepEd works. They would not see how the Bangsamoro Government then appropriates that money through the Bangsamoro Appropriations Act, how ministries obligate it, or who wins Bangsamoro contracts. Those records stay where they are today: scattered across ministry pages, PhilGEPS postings and COA reports.

## Can the Bangsamoro adopt the same?

Yes. Nothing stops the Bangsamoro Parliament from enacting a regional CADENA, and in one respect it could go further than the national bill.

The Bangsamoro Organic Law gives the Parliament legislative power over the region's own budgeting and administrative organisation, and gives the Bangsamoro Government supervision over its constituent provinces, cities and municipalities. A disclosure law is squarely within that. The Parliament already legislates the Bangsamoro Appropriations Act every year and has passed its own Administrative Code and Local Governance Code; a transparency act that tells ministries and offices what to post, where, and by when is the same kind of law. The national bill itself anticipates this: Section 13 lets the DICT, DBM and DILG lend hosting, training and technical help to governments that voluntarily adopt CADENA or build something like it.

A Bangsamoro version would cover the records CADENA leaves out:

| Stage | Bangsamoro records a regional act could require | Who would post |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation | Ministry budget proposals, the Chief Minister's budget message, the proposed Bangsamoro Expenditure Program | MFBM, ministries |
| Legislation | Every version of the appropriations bill, committee reports, amendments with proponents, the enacted BAA | Parliament, Office of the Speaker |
| Execution | Allotment and cash releases to ministries and offices, obligations, disbursement vouchers, Special Development Fund and block grant utilisation | MFBM, ministries |
| Procurement | Program of works, bids, awards, contracts, progress billings and completion for every Bangsamoro procuring entity | Each ministry's head of procuring entity |
| Accountability | COA audit reports on the Bangsamoro Government, ministry action plans, Parliament oversight records | COA, ministries, Parliament |

The gap in the national bill is where the regional one could be stronger. CADENA excludes LGUs because Congress chose not to reach them. The Bangsamoro Government, which supervises its LGUs directly through the MILG, could bring provincial and municipal budgets and contracts under the same portal from the start, or phase them in the way the national bill phases in agencies.

Three things a Bangsamoro act could not change. COA remains the auditor; a regional law can require audit reports to be posted but cannot alter the audit. Procurement in the region still runs under the national procurement law and PhilGEPS; a regional act layers disclosure on top of those rules rather than replacing them. And the national block grant is released by the DBM, so the upstream release documents would live on the national portal, not the regional one. The two systems would need to link rather than duplicate, which is exactly what the interoperability clauses in CADENA are written for.

The harder question is political will, not legal power. The national bill moved only after a scandal made inaction costly. Whether the Parliament takes this up before or after Congress does is a choice its members get to make, and one the public can press them on.

## What this means for BetterBARMM

BetterBARMM already does, by hand and after the fact, a small part of what a Bangsamoro CADENA would do by law and in real time. The Budget workspace holds the enacted appropriations acts from FY 2020 to FY 2026. The Legislation workspace tracks what the Parliament passes. The Procurement workspace gathers what Bangsamoro agencies post on PhilGEPS, with the honest caveat that the data may be incomplete because posting is uneven.

That caveat is the point. A portal built by a citizen can only show what the government has chosen to publish, in whatever format it chose. A disclosure law flips the default: the record is posted within seven days or someone is liable, and it arrives in a structured format that a site like this one can pull through an open API instead of scraping and retyping. CADENA even provides for accredited mirror sites run by universities, media and civil society, so that the official record does not sit on a single government server.

Until either Congress or the Parliament acts, this portal will keep doing the slower version. If a Bangsamoro CADENA is ever filed, the records already gathered here are a fair preview of what it should require.
