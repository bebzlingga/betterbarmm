AN ORDINANCE PROVIDING FOR A CHILD WELFARE AND PROTECTION CODE
        OF THE MUNICIPALITY OF NUEVA ERA, ILOCOS NORTE.


       WHEREAS, in its commitment to the Convention of the Rights of the Child, the
Government of the Philippines adopted the Philippine National Strategic Framework for Plan
Development for Children (2000-2025), or briefly called as “CHILD 21” , which outlines the
goals and strategies to realize the vision of the Filipino Child within the first quarter of the 21st
Century;

       WHEREAS, “Child 21” binds the national and local governments to synchronize
development plans and operations with the child as the focal beneficiary, hence the need of
mainstreaming the child rights in local development planning;

        WHEREAS, “Child 21” further states the need to be sensitive to the needs of the child
by building a Child-Friendly Society where children are nurtured and allowed to grow and
develop in dignity, protected from threats to their well-being and free to express themselves and
participate in their development;

        WHEREAS, national laws, rules and regulations promulgated for the welfare and
protection of children need to be strengthened at the local levels by also adopting supportive and
enabling local rules, policies and approaches;

       NOW, THEREFORE, be it enacted by the Sangguniang Bayan of Nueva Era, llocos
Norte, that:

                                           Article I
                                      TITLE AND POLICY

      Section 1. Title. This Ordinance shall be known as the CHILD WELFARE AND
PROTECTION CODE OF NUEVA ERA, ILOCOS NORTE, briefly referred to herein as the
Code.

        Section 2. Statement of Policy. Consistent with, and guided by, all existing national
laws, policies, as well as conventions to which the Government is a party, regarding the
development and protection of children, it is hereby declared to be the policy of the municipality
to consider itself both as the agent and partner of the Government of the Philippines insofar as
their implementation and in furtherance thereto.

         It is the policy of the municipality to always regard children as its fragile, delicate human
resources highly deserving utmost care. Towards this end, it shall always include in all phases of
its local development plans, programs and investments for the welfare, benefits and protection in
keeping faith with the generally -accepted notion that “the youth are the hope of the nation.”

                             ARTICLE II
     LOCAL COUNCIL FOR THE WELFARE AND PROTECTION OF CHILDREN

        Section 3. Creation of a Council for the Welfare and Protection of Children. There
is hereby created a Local Council for the Welfare and Protection of Children, referred to in this
Code as the Council, which shall comprised of the following:

         1. Chairperson : Municipal Mayor
         2. Municipal Committee Chair on Women and Family
         3. Municipal Planning and Development Coordinator
         4. Municipal Social Welfare Officer
         5. Municipal School Principal
         6. Municipal Civil Registrar
         7. Municipal Counsel
         8. Municipal Federation of SK President
         9. Municipal Health Officer
         10.            Municipal Agriculture Officer
         11. ABC President
         12. Municipal Treasurer
         13. Municipal Nutrition Action Officer
         14. Municipal President of Day Care Workers
         15. Four Religious Sector Representatives
         16.            Chief of Police
         17. Municipal Local Government Operations Officer

     Section 3. Powers and Functions of the Council. The Council shall have the following as
its powers and functions.
     1) Formulate Annual Action Plan for Children and submit the same to the Municipal
         Development Council and, upon approval, shall cause for its implementation;
     2) Take cognizant of every issue, case or incident of child abuse of whatever form, reported
         to any office or agency within or outside the municipality, and deliberate as to
         appropriate action or assistance deem needed, or render recommendations thereto, or
         initiate action if needed and proper;
     3) Monitor documents such issues, cases or incidents reported or referred to it, or acted
         upon, and render progress or statistical reports annually or as often as necessary or
         appropriate decided by the Council;
     4) Initiate, conduct, sponsor or participate in child-related for a or related assembly, as well
         as attend similar events either local, national or international gatherings, and may
         conduct fund-raising drives or campaigns to finance such adopted projects and activities;
     5) Coordinate with all other sectors, office or organization, including those offices
         represented in the Council, by consolidating all their child-related or children-oriented
         programs and activities to form part of the integrated local children-friend program;
     6) Institutionalize child-related projects or activities prescribed in this Code, including
         those the Council may so adopt; I                                                      Zj

        towards the welfare and protection of children in the municipality;

     Section 5. Meetings. The Council shall have a regular meeting of at least once every
quarter, and may convene special meetings as often as may be called by the Chairman, or at the
instance of at least three (3) members. Under no instance the Council shall meet outside the
territorial jurisdiction of the municipality.

    Section 6. Executive Officer & Secretariat. Upon recommendation of the Council, the
Municipal Mayor shall designate an official as Executive Officer of the Council. The designated
executive officer must be occupying a permanent position in the municipal organizational
structure, capable and willing, and whose duties and responsibilities are related to the powers and
services of the Council.

   The Council shall promulgate the duties and responsibilities of the Executive Officer:
Provided, that the following shall be deemed included:
       1) Secretary of the Council;
       2) Administer projects and activities of the Council;
       3) Liaison Officer of the Council with any office or entity;
       4) Monitor implementation of the provisions of this Code and those promulgated and
           directed by the Council.

       Section 7. Principal Office. The Municipal Hall shall serve as the principal office of
the council.
                                       Article III
                           CHILD SERVICE VOLUNTEERS

        Section 8. Child Service Volunteers. The Local Council for the Welfare & Protection
of Children shall deputize and designate at least one Child Service Volunteer in every barangay,
school, including commercial establishments, church, other entities and organization as may be
determined by the Council.

        A volunteer must at least be a high school graduate, able and willing to assist the Council
and advocate child welfare and protection: Provided, however,That a resident teacher, a social
worker, a barangay Health Worker or a day Care Worker may be given preference in the case of
the barangays.

    Section 9. Duties and Functions of Child Service Volunteers. The following are the
duties and functions of a volunteer:

   1) Serve as the Official Coordinator of the Council in his or her area;
   2) Monitor and report to the Council at a regular or periodic basis the existence and nature
      of every case directly or indirectly involving a child or children, any incident of child
      abuse, including benefits being given by, or program or project adopted and implemented
      for the welfare, development and protection of a child or children in his or her area,
      organization or entity, as the case may be;                                         d

                                      Article IV
                          ANNUAL ACTION PLAN FOR CHILDREN

       Section 11. Annual Action Plan for Children. Not later than September of every year,
   the Council shall formulate and adopt an Annual Action Plan for Children for the following
   year which shall consist of the following:

   1) the calendar of activities that are to be undertaken, the targeted or expected participants
      and beneficiaries, the nature and significance, the lead agency, and its proposed
      budgetary requirement;
   2) The projects to be carried out or be undertaken, its nature and significance, the lead
      agency or office, and its proposed funding requirement;
   3) The programs to be launched, its nature and significance, targeted or expected
      participants and beneficiaries, the lead agency, and its funding requirement.

    In the formulation of action plans, the Council shall always include and institutionalize those
prescribed in this Code, and those inter-sectoral commitments submitted by the members of the
Council.

    Section 12. Plan Requirements and Standards. In the formulation and development of the
component programs, projects and activities of the action plans, the Council shall be guided by,
and towards the accomplishment of, the “Child 21’s” goals and the requirements and standards
to achieve children’s health, nutrition, education, protection and participation in socio-cultural
and development activities as formulated by the Council for the Welfare of Children. (Table 1).

    Section 13. Mainstreaming into the Municipal Investment/Development Plan. The
action plan shall be submitted to the Municipal Development Council (MDC) for integration to
the proposed Annual Municipal Development /Investment Plan for the following year.

    The Municipal Development/investment Plan shall not be submitted to the Municipal
Development Council unless the action plan be integrated thereof, and the Sanggunianag Bayan,
in its approval pursuant to the Local Government Code, shall see to it that such action plan had
been duly considered and mainstreamed therein.

    Any particular item or items in the action plan not integrated               in the Annual
Development/investment Plan shall be the responsibility of the Council to find alternative means
for its implementation.

    Section 14. Duty of the Council Chairman and Executive Officer. It is t he duty of the
Chairman and the Executive Officer of the Council to submit such action plan and for its
integration into the investment or development plans of the municipality.

    It is likewise their duty to monitor the implementation of its approved component programs,
projects and activities, and to report to the Conned their progress status and monitoring reports.

                                  Article V
                INVENTORY OF CHILDREN AND PREGNANT WOMEN

Section 15. Inventory of Children and Pregnant Women. The Council, in coordination with
the Municipal Health Office, the barangay councils, and through Child Service Volunteers, shall
conduct a continuing inventory and maintain a profile of children and pregnant women
population of the municipality on a quarterly basis.

        The updated profile for children shall be in an appropriate statistical presentation,
showing the number of birth and death, age bracket or groupings, those in-school and out-of-
school, health profile, and other data the Council may require, such as abnormalities of children.

Section 16. Computer-aided Profile and Data Generation. The Council shall develop a
computer-aided information system that process and generate data, and the management of such
system shall be user-friendly and generally accessible, subject only to regulations and policies
prescribed therefore.

Section 17. Reporting of Births and Pregnancies. Subject to the recommendations of the
Council, all sangguniang barangays shall promulgate their respective ordinance requiring the
reporting of every child birth and pregnant women to their respective Child Service Volunteers
who in return submits a validated summary report to the Council Secretariat.

Section 18. Data and Reports Analysis. The Council shall periodically analyze profiles and
reports and render its recommendations to the Municipal Mayor, and such recommendations
must be related with, or toward the realization of, the Child 21 standards and requirements. A
recommendation may either pertains to general case or problem affecting a number or group of
children or to a particular child.

                                      Article VI
                          CIVIL REGISTRATION OF CHILDREN

Section 19. Registration of Every Child. It shall be the responsibility of the parent(s) or
guardian residing or staying in the municipality to register a child at birth with the Municipal
Civil Registrar, or which must be not later that one (1) week from birth.

Any information or unregistered child that reaches to the knowledge of the Child Service
Volunteer shall, after verification, be reported by him or her to the Municipal Civil Registrar
who in return shall cause the registration.

Section 20. Birth or Delivery of Non-resident in the municipality. It is a mandatory that if a
non-resident choose to deliver, or a child was bom in the municipality, the concerned parent
shall register the child with the Municipal Registrar of Nueva Era..

Section 21. Mass Civil Registration of Children. Upon the effectivity of this Code, the
Municipal Civil Registrar shall conduct at least twice a year (preferably on the 1st week of
February and October ] a barangay-based outreach child registration services.

Section 23. Child Birth Certificate. The Municipal Civil Registrar shall issue not earlier than
fifteen days from registration and free of charge a copy of certificate of live birth of every child
registered with its office: Provided, However, That any certificate secured thereafter shall be
paid in accordance with the Revenue Code of the Municipality/^

                                 Article VII
                  CHILDREN HEALTH ASSISTANCE AND SERVICES

Section 24. Immunization of Children. It shall be the responsibility of every parent of
guardian to cause the expanded program on immunization of his/her child against BCG,
tuberculosis, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, oral polio vaccine (OPV)/pneumococal conjugate
vaccine (PCV) measles, mumps, rubella (MMR) and hepatitis B with the Municipal Health
Office.

If the immunization shall be done by any medical practitioner or institution other than the
Municipal Health Office, the concerned parents or guardian shall secure a proof of compliance
thereof and which shall be available for any authorized verifications.

The concerned Child Service Volunteer is authorized to conduct periodic verification of such
proofs of immunization of all children in his or her area.

Section 25. Vitamin Supplementation/Micronutrient Supplementation. It shall likewise be
the responsibility of every parent or guardian to give one (1) dose of Vitamin A starting at 6-11
months and 1-5 years old are given Vitamin A capsules twice a year and micronutrient
supplementation for prevention of underweight, anemia, and iron deficiency disorder to
children.

Section 26. Child Health Awareness Week/bservance of Children’s Week Celebration.
Upon coordination with the Council which shall be not later than one (1) month from the
approval of this Code, the Municipal Mayor shall, by appropriate issuance therefore, promulgate
and institutionalize a Child Health Awareness Week in the municipality. The weeklong
observance shall extend a package of health services for children to include, but not limited to,
the following:
    I) mass immunization
    2) mass vitamin supplementation;
    3) medical, surgical, dental and visual examinations and services;
    4) special supplemental feeding;
    5) child-oriented lectures, preferably on the subjects of anti-illegal drugs, smoking,
        prevailing child health risk;
    6) presentation of Researchers and related forum;
    7) launching special projects benefiting children;
    8) Institutionalized programs of the Department of Health.
    9) Physical fitness/sports activities for healthy lifestyle.

During the observance, the municipality may tap the services or mobilize the participation and
involvement of other entities, medical practitioners, hospitals or clinics, schools, barangay
volunteers and interested organizations.

The celebration of the children and youth week is done every 3rd week of October in the
municipality and the observance of the National Children’s week celebration is done every
month of November.

Section 27. Pregnant Women Health Day. Upon recommendation of the Council, the
Municipal Mayor shall, by appropriate issuance, promulgate and institutionalize a Pregnant
Women Health Day once a month. During such day, the Local Government Unit thru the
Municipal Health Office shall provide or extend package of health services to pregnant women,
such as pre-natal consultation, pregnancy orientation/lectures, and laboratory services.

In the implementation, the municipality, through the said offices, may tap or mobilize the
participation of interested individual or group medical practitioner (s), clinic or hospitals, to
undertake their services inside the barangay health stations and RHU Lying in clinic , subject to
appropriate arrangement.                                                                        4

Section 28. Grave Health cases and Abnormalities. The Council shall take cognizant, and
after deliberation, decide a form of assistance to any child suffering from grave health ailment or
abnormalities. In every case, and based on a case study conducted therefore by the Municipal
Health Office and Municipal Social welfare Office, the Council shall adopt appropriate package
of assistance services which include referral services, hospitalization assistance or financial
assistance. In all cases, the Council shall respect the religious belief of the family and maintain
confidentiality of identity of the child.

Section 29. Supplemental Feeding Program. Within six (6) months from the effectivity of
this Code, the Municipal Health Office, Municipal Nutrition Action Officer and the Municipal
Social Welfare Office shall rationalize the supplemental feeding program of the municipality by
formulating and adopting an implementing guidelines therefore. The program shall outline,
among others, the following components:

   1) objective or problem (s) to be solved;
   2) beneficiaries;
   3) procedures of the feeding program;
   4) sources of funds and supplies;
   5) results evaluation system.

                               Article VIII
           CHILDREN EARLY EDUCATIONAL DEVELOPMENT SERVICES

Section 30. Municipal Day Care Services and Pre-School Programs. It shall be the
responsibility of every parent or guardian to have his or her child who is from three (3) to six (6)
years old to attend an early education program which include the day care services and the pre­
school programs of the barangay and the municipality: Provided, That whenever possible, the
child shall be enrolled to the Child Development Center established in the barangay where he or
she resides.

Towards this purpose, the concerned Child Service Volunteer shall include in his monitoring and
reporting, responsibilities the children ranging from three (3) to six (6) years old who are not
enrolled in any accredited Day care Center or Pre-school, as the case may be, and submit the
same to the Municipal Social Welfare Office and to the Council, indicating therein the concerned
parents or guardian.

Section 31. Pre-schools Monitoring Reports. Effective upon approval of this Code, the
Municipal Social Welfare office shall establish and maintain an integrated monitoring chart and
reports covering all child development centers and pre-schools (public or private] in the
municipality.

The monitoring chart and report shall contain or indicate, but not limited to, the following:

   1) name of center or schools and its location;
   2) owner or administrator of the center or school;
   3) enrollment population;
   4) teacher or its equivalent;
   5) official schedule of classes.

It shall also secure a certified copy of the center’s or school’s policy of instructions or similar

Section 32. Inspection of Centers and Pre-schools. Notwithstanding the inspection and

                                    Article IX
                      IN-SCHOOL CHILDREN SUPPORT SERVICES

Section 35. School Children Assistance and Incentive Fund. The Municipal School Board
shall establish in its annual budget a School children Assistance and Incentive Fund, the amount
of which shall be recommended by the District Supervisor in coordination with the
representative of Integrated Parent-Teachers & Community Association (PTCA).

The Board shall promulgate the implementing guidelines, but which necessarily include or based
on the following parameters:

   1) The Assistance Fund component to subsidize classroom needs of children belonging to
      the indigent families, which include basic school supplies and textbooks;
   2) The Incentive Fund component to finance or grant incentives, in the form of cash or
      otherwise, to deserving pupils in academics, sports, scouting, leadership, and other
      extraordinary achievements. It shall also extend to teaching and non-teaching officials or
      personnel, who had shown or done outstanding achievement.

Every assistance or incentive to be granted shall be decided by the Board upon presentation and
consideration of the proper justification therefore the Fund, or any unexpended balance thereof,
shall not be used or realigned for any other purpose without proper consultation with the officers
or representative of the integrated PTCA and a duly recognized organization of teaching and
non-teaching personnel of the municipality.

Section 36. Institutionalized LGU Medal Awards. Starting from the school year which this
Code shall take effect, the Municipal School Board shall provide a LGU-specially designed
medals to be awarded to pupils and students belonging to the honor roll of all levels in all
schools in the municipality. The design of the medal awards shall be institutionalized and shall
not be modified unless otherwise authorized by appropriate ordinance.

Section 37. LGU Scholarship Program. The Sangguniang Bayan, in coordination with the
Municipal School Board, shall, within a reasonable time after the approval of this Code, establish
a Scholarship Program for Children of the municipality./A                                         £

                                     Article X
                         OUT-OF-SCHOOL CHILDREN SERVICES

Section 38. Alternative Education Services. The Municipal School Board shall cause the
adoption and integration into the local non-formal education program a barangay-based
alternative education services for out-of-school children, which include maintenance of reading
centers in every barangay.

Section 39. Employment Assistance. Upon approval of this Code, the municipal Public
Employment Service Office (PESO) shall formulate an employment assistance projects of out-
of-school children and incorporate in its annual program, subject to applicable labor standards or
regulations for working children.


                                    Article XI
                    SPECIAL SUPPORT PROGRAMS FOR CHILDREN

Section 40. Annual Children’s Assembly. The last day of the declared Children Awareness
Week pursuant to an order issued therefore shall be considered as annual assembly for children,
known thereof as “Children’s Festival. The Council shall formulate and adopt the features of the
annual assembly.

Section 41. Bright Child. The Council shall institutionalize an annual search and recognition
of gifted children of the municipality, known thereof as “Bright Child of the Year”, and provide
incentives therefore.

Section 42. Fund Drives for Children. Subject to the prior presentation and adoption of a
project or activity design therefore, any fund raising campaigns or drives where the proceeds
thereof are exclusively and directly for the benefits of children of the Municipality of Nueva Era
initiated by any barangay or organization shall be exempted of permit fees, notwithstanding to
the provision of an ordinance to the contrary.

Section 43. Specific Prohibited Acts or Treatment of Children; Penalties. While within the
territorial jurisdiction of the municipality, the following are prohibited acts or omissions with
their corresponding sanctions or penalties:

   1) deliberately or negligently allowing a child of three (3) years or below roaming or
      playing alone in a public street or road;
   2) deliberately or negligently allowing a child regardless of age or sex roaming or playing
      outside of residence naked.

Any person may report such erring parent or guardian for violating any or both of the above acts
or omission and, upon verification, the Council shall call the attention in person of the parents or
guardian for child care orientation.

   3) transporting by vehicle a child of ten (10) years old or below be improperly loaded. For
      the purpose of this Code, a child is improperly loaded when she or she is not safety
      seated inside the vehicle.

   Penalty - a fine of two hundred pesos (P200.00)

                                          Article XII
                                      FINAL PROVISIONS

Section 44. Reports. Not later than the thirtieth day of March of the following year, the
Council shall render its annual report as to the implementation of this Code, particularly
including its accomplishments, observations and recommendations.

Section 45. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Code is declared invalid or
unconstitutional by appropriate agency or office, the remaining provisions not affected thereby
shall continue in full force and effect.

Section 46. Repealing Clause. All Ordinances, resolutions and other issuances inconsistent
with the provision of this Code are hereby repealed and modified accordingly.

Section 47. Effectivity Clause. This Code shall take effect upon review and approval of the
Sangguniang Panlalawigan and after posting in conspicuous places of the municipality.

including its accomplishments, observations and recommendations.

Section 45. Separability Clause. If any provision of this Code is declared invalid or
unconstitutional by appropriate agency or office, the remaining provisions not affected thereby
shall continue in full force and effect.

Section 46. Repealing Clause. All Ordinances, resolutions and other issuances inconsistent
with the provision of this Code are hereby repealed and modified accordingly.

Section 47. Effectivity Clause. This Code shall take effect upon review and approval of the
Sangguniang Panlalawigan and after posting in conspicuous places of the municipality.
