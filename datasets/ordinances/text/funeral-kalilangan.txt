WHEREAS, the Department of Health, through Administrative Order No. 2020-0008,
prescribes the rules on the Medical Certification of Cause of Death (MCCOD), including
standard procedures for reporting, certifying, and reviewing the cause of death, as well as
the responsibilities of certifiers, health facilities, and local health offices in ensuring
complete and accurate certification of deaths (DOH AO 2020-0008, pp. 1-2) ;

WHEREAS, the Department of Health Administrative Order No. 2020-0008 requires
that only the attending physician who has directly examined the patient, or the Municipal
Health Officer who has personally assessed the deceased or conducted a Verbal Autopsy in
accordance with national standards, may certify the fact and cause of death; and that no
physician may lawfully declare or certify death without personal examination or the required
medico-legal procedures, consistent with the national rules on Medical Certification of Cause
of Death;

WHEREAS, the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) mandates
LGUs to ensure compliance with national public health standards, including proper sanitation
of funeral establishments, accurate civil registration processes, and enforcement of health
regulations within their local jurisdictions;

WHEREAS, Republic Act No. 12160, otherwise known as the “Philippine Islamic Burial
Act,” mandates the proper and immediate burial of Muslim cadavers in accordance with
Islamic rites, allows burial with or without a death certificate, and prescribes post-burial
reporting and certification procedures;

WHEREAS, accurate and timely declaration of death is essential for public health
monitoring, civil registration, and legal processes such as succession, insurance claims, and
settlement of estates;

WHEREAS, the absence of a clear local procedure for death declaration may result in
delays, fraudulent claims, and administrative inefficiencies;

WHEREAS, delays and inconsistencies in death declaration and certification can lead
to unregistered deaths, inaccurate mortality statistics, delays in legal processes, and
potential administrative issues concerning families and institutions;

WHEREAS, the cultural and religious practices of Muslim and Indigenous Peoples (IP)
communities—especially the custom of immediate burial—must be respected, while
ensuring timely reporting and certification of death as allowed under national guidelines;

WHEREAS, funeral homes must comply with DOH sanitation standards, employ
licensed embalmers, and ensure that no embalming is performed without a proper death
declaration;

WHEREAS, the Local Government Code of 1991 empowers local government units to
enact ordinances promoting public health, safety, and welfare, including the regulation of
death declaration and funeral home operations;

WHEREAS, it is necessary to adopt localized procedures consistent with DOH AO
2020-0008 and DILG guidance, while reflecting actual operational practices in Kalilangan,
including the established system where families commonly notify the Local Disaster Risk
Reduction and Management Office (LDRRMO), which then coordinates the deployment of
the Municipal Health Officer (MHO) for on-site examination and declaration;

SECTION 1. TITLE. This ordinance shall be known as the “Death Declaration and
Funeral Home Regulation Ordinance of Kalilangan.”

SECTION 2. DECLARATION OF POLICY. It is hereby declared the policy of the
Municipality of Kalilangan to ensure the proper, legal, and dignified handling of deceased
persons within its jurisdiction. This ordinance seeks to protect public health and safety,
uphold the rights and dignity of the deceased and their families, and regulate the operations
of funeral homes and related entities. The Municipality shall implement these regulations in
accordance with existing national laws, Department of Health (DOH) issuances, and the

oO powers granted under the Local Government Code of 1991.

SECTION 3. DEFINITION OF TERMS. Terms used in this ordinance shall mean:

1. Death Declaration — The formal pronouncement of death by a licensed physician or
the Municipal Health Officer (MHO), including the determination of the date, time,
and cause of death.

2. Death Certificate - An official document issued by the Local Civil Registrar based
on the declaration of death, containing details such as the deceased’s identity, cause
of death, and certifying authority.

3. Certificate of Death Declaration - A formal certification issued by the Municipal

Health Officer or attending physician confirming that death has been declared. This

an certificate shall serve as the official basis for funeral homes to verify that the death
has been properly declared prior to accepting or embalming the body.

4. Municipal Health Officer (MHO) - The licensed physician designated by the local
government to oversee public health services and authorized to declare deaths within
the municipality.

5. Attending Physician - A licensed medical doctor who is present at the time of death
or who has provided medical care to the deceased prior to death. The attending
physician is authorized to declare death and issue the Certificate of Death Declaration
in accordance with applicable laws and regulations.

6. Barangay Health Emergency Response Team (BHERT) - A barangay-based
team composed of health workers and barangay officials responsible for responding
to health-related emergencies, including coordination of death declarations.

7. Funeral Home - Any establishment engaged in the business of embalming, storing,
or preparing deceased persons for burial or cremation.

8. Embalming - The process of preserving a deceased body using chemical substances,

which must be performed only by a licensed embalmer and after a proper death
declaration.

9. Verbal Autopsy - A method used to determine the probable cause of death through

attending physician are available.

10.LDRRMO (Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office) - A
municipal office responsible for disaster preparedness and emergency response,
which may assist in coordinating death declarations during emergencies or in remote
areas.

11. Medical Certification of Cause of Death (MCCOD) - The completion by a licensed
physician of the medical certification portion of the death certificate indicating the
cause of death based on best medical opinion and available records/information.

12. Certifier — A licensed physician authorized to certify the fact of death and to certify
the circumstances (accident, suicide, homicide, natural causes) and the specific
disease, injury, or other cause(s) of death.

13.Medically Attended Death - A death wherein the deceased was attended by a
licensed physician during the last illness immediately prior to death or within the
twelve (12) months preceding death.

14. Medically Unattended Death - A death wherein the deceased was not attended
by a licensed physician during the last illness immediately prior to death or within the
twelve (12) months preceding death.

A“ 15. In-Facility Death — A death that occurred in a health facility, including emergency
room deaths.

16. Out-of-Facility Death - A death that occurred outside a health facility such as in a
home, public area, building, or moving conveyance.

17.Dead-on-Arrival (DOA) - A case where a patient is brought to a health facility
without cardio-pulmonary and brain functions and is declared dead on arrival,
excluding decapitation not susceptible to resuscitation and bodies in advanced
decomposition.

18. Medico-Legal Case - A death requiring investigation or autopsy/examination by
medico-legal officers of PNP/NBI or other authorized investigative agencies.

19. Certificate of Fetal Death (COFD) - The PSA-prescribed form used for registration
of fetal death, subject to applicable national rules.

SECTION 4. COVERAGE. This ordinance shall apply to all deaths occurring within
the territorial jurisdiction of Kalilangan, whether in hospitals, homes, or public places, and
to all funeral homes operating therein.

SECTION 5. GENERAL RULES ON DECLARATION OF DEATH AND
CERTIFICATION (BY TYPE OF DEATH).

A. GENERAL RULE. All deaths must be declared by a licensed physician or the Municipal
Health Officer (MHO). In cases of death at home without an attending physician, the family
must immediately notify the Barangay Health Emergency Response Team (BHERT), the
MHO, or the Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (LDRRMO). The LDRRMO
shall coordinate with the MHO or BHERT to facilitate timely death declaration. The MHO shall
conduct an on-site examination and issue a Certificate of Death Declaration. If the MHO is
unavailable, the body may be transferred to the nearest appropriate medical facility where
a licensed physician may examine and declare the death in accordance with applicable laws
and regulations.

B. IN-FACILITY DEATHS

For medically unattended deaths:

1) The nearest relative or person with knowledge of the death shall report to the MHO
usually through LDRRMO or BHERT.

2. The MHO shall conduct an on-site assessment or, if necessary, Verbal Autopsy to certify
the cause of death, unless characteristics indicate a medico-legal case.

E. EMERGENCY ROOM (ER) / EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT DEATHS

1) For deaths occurring or brought to the ER, the ER physician-on-duty shall certify the
cause of death, except medico-legal cases.

2) For DOA cases that do not require medico-legal investigation, verbal autopsy may be
conducted to certify cause of death.

F. DEATHS IN AN AMBULANCE OR DURING TRANSFER

1) If death occurs in an ambulance with a physician present, the physician present during
transport or the receiving facility’s authorized physician shall certify.

2) If death occurs in an ambulance without a physician and/or without referral, the case
may be treated as DOA and handled accordingly, subject to medico-legal assessment.

G. DEATHS IN MOVING CONVEYANCES. The driver/pilot (or owner, if no survivors) shall
report the death to the MHO of the city/municipality where the death occurred, who shall
certify the cause of death with available information and best medical opinion.

H. FETAL DEATHS. Certification and registration of fetal deaths shall follow applicable
national rules, including when certification is mandatory based on gestational age/weight
as provided in national issuances.

I. DEATHS REQUIRING MEDICO-LEGAL INVESTIGATION. Deaths that meet medico-
legal criteria shall be referred immediately to PNP/NBI/authorized medico-legal officer
pursuant to Section 8 hereof.

In accordance with Republic Act No. 12160, the death of a Filipino Muslim shall be

within fourteen (14) days after burial to the Municipal Health Officer (MHO), who shall

reported to the Office of the Municipal Mayor, who may prepare and certify the death

Funeral homes, hospitals, and other custodial facilities shall respect religious and
cultural burial practices and shall not impede immediate burial. Non-payment of hospital
bills, professional fees, or funeral expenses shall not be used as a ground to withhold the
release of the remains.

For documentation purposes, a written confirmation from a duly recognized Imam,
Islamic religious leader, or authorized cultural leader attesting to the fact of death and burial
may be submitted to the Municipal Health Officer as supporting documentation. Such
confirmation shall not be considered a medical declaration of death but may be used by the
Municipal Health Officer as part of the post-burial assessment, documentation, and
certification process, including verbal autopsy when applicable.

K. AUTHORIZED SIGNATORY IN THE ABSENCE OF THE MUNICIPAL HEALTH
OFFICER (MHO). In accordance with Section 91 of Presidential Decree No. 856 (Code on
Sanitation of the Philippines) and existing civil registration rules, when the Municipal Health
Officer (MHO) or any licensed physician authorized to declare death is unavailable, the Local
Civil Registrar (LCR) may accept for registration a Certificate of Death or Certification of the
Fact of Death signed by any of the following municipal officials, strictly for purposes of civil
registration of death:

a) The Municipal Mayor;
b) The Secretary to the Sangguniang Bayan; or
c) Any incumbent Member of the Sangguniang Bayan.

Such authority shall apply only to the certification of the fact of death and shall not
include the Medical Certification of Cause of Death (MCCOD), which remains the exclusive
function of a licensed physician or the Municipal Health Officer in accordance with DOH
Administrative Order No. 2020-0008.

The exercise of this authority shall be subject to the following conditions: The death
does not involve any medico-legal or forensic interest;The certification is supported by
reliable documents such as barangay certification, affidavits of persons with personal
knowledge of the death, police or incident reports (when applicable), or post-burial
confirmation in the case of immediate burial; and The death certificate shall be subsequently
reviewed and medically completed by the Municipal Health Officer upon availability,
particularly for the purpose of indicating or validating the cause of death.

This provision is intended to prevent delay in death registration, respect cultural and

religious practices involving immediate burial, and ensure continuity of civil registry services
in the municipality.

SECTION 6. PROCEDURE FOR DECLARATION OF DEATH (LOCALIZED
TIMELINES) AND REGISTRATION FLOW. To ensure timely, lawful, and efficient
declaration and registration of deaths while respecting cultural and religious practices, the
following procedures shall be observed:

1. Reporting of Death

a. All deaths occurring within the Municipality of Kalilangan shall be reported
to the Municipal Health Office (MHO) or the nearest health facility as soon as
practicable.

b. For medically unattended deaths, reporting shall be made at the earliest
practicable time to allow timely assessment and certification by the Municipal
Health Officer.

c. In the case of Filipino Muslims who practice immediate burial in accordance
with Islamic rites, burial may proceed with or without a death certificate;
Provided, That the death shall be reported within fourteen (14) days after
burial to the Municipal Health Officer, or to the Office of the Municipal Mayor
in the absence or unavailability of the MHO, in accordance with Republic Act
No. 12160.

2. Medical Certification
a) A licensed physician, including the attending physician, emergency room
physician-on-duty, hospital or clinic administrator who is a licensed
physician, or the Municipal Health Officer, shall accomplish the Medical
Certification of Cause of Death (MCCOD), indicating the date, time, and
cause of death based on best medical judgment and available records.

b) In cases where no medical records are available and the death was medically
unattended, the Municipal Health Officer shall conduct a Verbal Autopsy in
accordance with applicable national standards, unless the case requires
medico-legal investigation.

3. Mandatory Review Prior to Registration
a) The informant or nearest relative shall submit the duly reviewed death
certificate to the Local Civil Registrar within the reglementary period
prescribed by law.

b) Registration beyond the reglementary period shall be treated as delayed
registration and processed in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and
regulations.

4. Civil Registry Filing
a) The informant or nearest relative shall submit the duly reviewed death

certificate to the Local Civil Registrar within the reglementary period
prescribed by law.

b) Registration beyond the reglementary period shall be treated as delayed
registration and processed in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and
regulations.

5. Documentation and Records Management

The Municipal Health Office shall maintain a complete and updated registry of
all deaths declared and certified within the municipality, including the mode of
certification, and shall coordinate with the Local Civil Registrar and other
concerned offices for public health monitoring, planning, and compliance,
subject to data privacy and confidentiality requirements.

SECTION 7. FUNERAL HOME REGULATIONS. No funeral home shall accept, handle,
store, transport, prepare, or embalm any deceased person without a duly signed Certificate
of Death Declaration or a Medical Certification of Cause of Death issued by the Municipal
Health Officer or an attending licensed physician. Funeral homes shall verify the authenticity
and completeness of such documents before providing any service.

Embalming shall be performed strictly and exclusively by a duly licensed embalmer. Under
no circumstance shall any unlicensed individual, helper, trainee, or attendant conduct or
assist in the embalming process. Funeral homes shall ensure that their licensed embalmers
possess valid credentials and comply with all required standards of practice.

All funeral homes shall at all times maintain full compliance with Department of Health
(DOH) sanitation and safety standards. Such standards include proper disinfection and
cleaning of embalming areas, safe handling of bodies, appropriate storage and disposal of
hazardous and infectious waste, use of personal protective equipment (PPE), and
maintenance of structurally sound, hygienic, and well-ventilated facilities.

Each funeral home shall maintain a complete and updated logbook containing the name and
identifying details of the deceased, date and time of arrival, source of the death declaration
or medical certification, embalming authorization, the name and license number of the
embalmer who performed the procedure, and the date and time of release or transfer of
the body.

It shall be unlawful for any funeral home to accept remains without proper documents, to
allow unlicensed individuals to embalm or assist in embalming, or to handle or process
remains using incomplete, falsified, or unverified documentation.

SECTION 8. ENFORCEMENT. The Municipal Health Office shall lead enforcement,
supported by the Business Permits and Licensing Office, BHERTs, and the LDRRMO. Routine
inspections shall be conducted quarterly, with spot checks initiated upon complaints or
reports. A hotline shall be established for reporting violations and coordinating death
declarations. Investigations shall be conducted with due process including written notice,
opportunity to respond, and right to appeal.

A. Delayed Registration. Registration of death beyond the reglementary period allowed
under national rules shall be treated as delayed registration.

B. Required Documents (General)

For delayed registration or cases where death was not declared timely, the Local Civil
Registrar may require:

1) Affidavit of delayed registration/explanation by the informant;

2) Barangay certification, as appropriate;

3) Affidavits of two (2) disinterested persons who witnessed or have personal knowledge of
the death;

4) PSA Negative Certification, when required under applicable procedures; and

5) Other documents required by PSA/LCR rules.

C. Post Facto Certification
In cases where a death was not declared or medically certified at the time of occurrence, or
the body was already buried or embalmed before proper documentation, the following shall

apply:

1) MUNICIPAL HEALTH OFFICER (MHO) Review
The Municipal Health Officer shall conduct a post facto assessment based on available
information, including written confirmation from a recognized Imam or religious leader,
an verbal accounts from relatives/witnesses; barangay certification; medical records (if any);
_ police blotter or incident reports (when applicable); and any supporting documentation
presented.

2) Use of Verbal Autopsy
For medically unattended deaths or cases where medical attendance cannot be verified, the
MHO shall conduct Verbal Autopsy (VA) following applicable standards, unless the case
requires medico legal investigation.

3) Post Facto MCCOD
After review, the MHO may issue a post facto MCCOD based on best medical judgment and

evidence available. The cause of death may be indicated as “undetermined” when evidence
is insufficient.

4) Medico Legal Referral When Required
If circumstances suggest possible injury, violence, poisoning, or suspicious cause, the case
shall be referred to PNP/NBI for medico legal examination.
The MHO shall not issue a post facto MCCOD in cases requiring medico legal autopsy unless
cleared by authorities.

5) Transmittal to Local Civil Registrar
The MHO shall endorse the post facto MCCOD, VA summary (when applicable), and
supporting documents to the Local Civil Registrar for delayed registration processing.

6) LOCAL CIVIL REGISTRAR (LCR) Action
The LCR shall evaluate the documents and process delayed registration following applicable
rules, including encoding, annotation as delayed registration, and forwarding to PSA within
standard transmittal cycles.

7) No Duplicate Certificates
The MHO and LCR shall ensure that no duplicate death certificates exist prior to approving
delayed registration. Any discovered duplicate or fraudulent certificates shall be referred to
authorities for investigation.

SECTION 10. USE OF VERBAL AUTOPSY TOOLS. In cases of out-of-facility deaths
where no medical records or attending physician are available, the Municipal Health Officer
may utilize standardized verbal autopsy tools, such as the SmartVA Auto-Analyse system,
to assist in determining the probable cause of death. The MHO shall conduct a structured
interview with the deceased's relatives or caregivers and use the tool’s suggested causes to
inform the death certification. However, the final declaration must be documented in writing
and signed by the MHO or attending physician, in accordance with DOH and PSA guidelines.

SECTION 11. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES (LOCALIZED
IMPLEMENTATION)

1. MUNICIPAL HEALTH OFFICER (MHO)

The Municipal Health Officer (MHO) shall certify medically unattended deaths,
conduct verbal autopsy when appropriate, and review all death certificates and
Certificates of Fetal Death prior to registration with the Local Civil Registrar. The MHO
shall maintain updated mortality databases and logs, prepare periodic mortality
summaries for monitoring and planning purposes, coordinate trainings for physicians
and relevant personnel on MCCOD, ICD compliance, and verbal autopsy procedures,
and ensure that all medico-legal cases are properly documented and referred to the
appropriate authorities.

2. ATTENDING PHYSICIAN, ER PHYSICIAN-ON-DUTY, OR HOSPITAL
ADMINISTRATOR
The attending physician, ER physician-on-duty, or hospital administrator who is a
licensed physician shall certify medically attended deaths and emergency room
deaths, ensure early identification of medico-legal cases, promptly refer such cases
to the proper authorities, and transmit all required medical documentation to the
MHO for review and further action.

3. LOCAL CIVIL REGISTRAR (LCR)
The Local Civil Registrar (LCR) shall accept for registration only those death
certificates that have been reviewed and cleared by the MHO. The LCR shall process
delayed registrations in coordination with the MHO, barangay officials, and
informants; ensure the integrity and accuracy of civil registry entries; prevent the
creation of duplicate or erroneous records; and transmit all registered documents to
the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) in accordance with established procedures.

4. BARANGAY OFFICIALS AND THE BARANGAY HEALTH EMERGENCY RESPONSE
TEAM (BHERT)
Barangay officials and the Barangay Health Emergency Response Team (BHERT) shall
assist families in reporting deaths to the MHO, coordinate promptly with the MHO or
LDRRMO as needed, and support the preparation of required documents such as
barangay certifications or witness affidavits. They shall also assist in verbal autopsy
interviews by facilitating access to reliable informants and providing relevant
community information, and shall participate in information Campaigns and
monitoring of compliance at the barangay level.

5. LOCAL DISASTER RISK REDUCTION AND MANAGEMENT OFFICE (LDRRMO)
The Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (LDRRMO) shall serve as
a support and coordination unit in cases of reported deaths, particularly during

families in fulfilling required reporting obligations.

autopsy procedures, medico-legal referral protocols, and culturally sensitive guidance for
Muslim and IP communities.

SECTION 13. DATA PRIVACY AND CONFIDENTIALITY. All death-related
information, including Medical Certification of Cause of Death (MCCOD), Certificates of
Death Declaration, Verbal Autopsy results, interview notes, and death registry documents,
shall be handled in accordance with the Data Privacy Act and relevant national issuances.
Such information shall be accessed only by authorized personnel of the Municipal Health
Office (MHO), Local Civil Registrar (LCR), LDRRMO, BHERT, or other offices as may be
necessary for the implementation of this Ordinance. Unauthorized disclosure, sharing, or
use of death-related personal information is strictly prohibited and shall subject the
responsible individual to administrative, civil, and/or criminal liability under applicable laws.

SECTION 14. UNCLAIMED REMAINS AND INDIGENT DEATH ASSISTANCE. In
cases of indigent deceased persons or unclaimed remains, the Municipal Health Office
(MHO) shall coordinate with the Municipal Social Welfare and Development Office (MSWDO)
for appropriate assistance, including documentation, temporary storage, and arrangements
for burial or cremation, subject to available funds and municipal guidelines. Funeral homes
receiving unclaimed bodies shall immediately notify the MHO and MSWDO. All actions taken
shall prioritize public health, dignity of the deceased, and humane disposition of remains.

SECTION 15. STANDARDIZED REPORTING AND DOCUMENTATION. The
Municipal Health Office (MHO) shall develop and prescribe standardized reporting forms for
use by the LDRRMO, BHERT, funeral homes, and other concerned offices in the reporting of
deaths, conduct of verbal autopsy, recording of medico-legal referrals, and documentation
of home deaths. All concerned offices shall use only the prescribed forms and shall submit
copies to the MHO within the time frames required by this Ordinance. The use of
standardized documentation aims to ensure accuracy, consistency, and compliance with
DOH and PSA requirements.

SECTION 16. DISASTER, OUTBREAK, AND MASS FATALITY COORDINATION.
In cases of disasters, disease outbreaks, mass casualty incidents, or any situation resulting
in multiple fatalities, the Municipal Health Office (MHO), LDRRMO, PNP, MSWDO, funeral
homes, and other concerned offices shall coordinate under the Incident Command System

SECTION 17. PROHIBITION ON WITHHOLDING REMAINS DUE TO NON-
PAYMENT. No hospital, clinic, funeral home, or mortuary establishment shall refuse to
release the remains of a deceased person due to non-payment of hospital bills, service
charges, or funeral fees. This prohibition applies to both the issuance of death documents
and the physical release of the body. Facilities may secure promissory notes or follow other
lawful billing procedures but shall not withhold remains or delay burial.

SECTION 18. PENALTIES. Any person, funeral home, or entity found violating the
provisions of this ordinance shall be subject to the following penalties:

1. First Offense: Written warning and mandatory attendance in an orientation or
compliance seminar conducted by the Municipal Health Office.

2. Second Offense: A fine not exceeding Two Thousand Pesos (2,000.00) and
temporary suspension of local business permit for a period not exceeding three (3)
days.

3. Third and Succeeding Offenses: A fine not exceeding Two Thousand Five Hundred
Pesos (P2,500.00) and recommendation for revocation of business permit, subject
to due process.

These penalties shall apply to individuals, funeral homes, and other entities operating
within the Municipality of Kalilangan, and shall be imposed without prejudice to any further
liability under applicable national laws.

SECTION 19. FUNDING AND APPROPRIATION. Funding for the implementation
of this ordinance shall be sourced from the existing budget of the Municipal Health Office
(MHO).

The MHO may allocate resources for operational expenses including, but not limited
to:

e Training and capacity-building activities

¢ Documentation and issuance of certificates

e Public information campaigns

e Logistical support for health personnel in remote barangays

e Monitoring and enforcement activities

Additional funding requirements, if any, shall be proposed through the regular budget
process and included in the Annual Investment Plan (AIP).

All funding requirements and expenditures arising from the implementation of this
Ordinance, including training, logistics, documentation supplies, transportation of the MHO
for home deaths, and support for indigent or unclaimed remains, shall comply with the rules
and regulations of the Commission on Audit (COA) and applicable guidelines of the
Department of Health (DOH) and the Department of the Interior and Local Government
(DILG). No expenditure shall be incurred unless duly authorized and allowable under
existing budgeting, accounting, and auditing rules.

SECTION 20. REPEALING CLAUSE. All ordinances, resolutions, and executive
orders inconsistent with this ordinance are hereby repealed or modified accordingly.
