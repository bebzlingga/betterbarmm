                                    EXPLANATORY NOTE

                      Gender equality and social inclusion (GESI) refer to efforts
          aimed at ensuring equal opportunities, rights, and treatment for all
          individuals, regardless of their gender, ethnicity, socio-economic status,
          age, disability, or other characteristics. The promotion of GESI is a crucial
          aspect of fostering a just and inclusive society, where everyone can fully
          participate in and benefit from social, economic, and political
          development. Gender equality and social inclusion (GESI) stand at the
          forefront of the Philippines' socio-economic and political agenda,
          reflecting a commitment to building a society that values and empowers
          all its citizens regardless of gender, ethnicity, socio-economic status, and
          other diversities. The pursuit of GESI is not merely a policy objective but
          a transformative journey toward creating a more just, inclusive, and
          equitable nation.

                     Promoting gender equality and social inclusion is an ongoing
          process that requires collaboration between government, civil society,
          and the private sector. By addressing systemic inequalities, empowering
          marginalized groups, and fostering an inclusive environment, the City
          can work towards building a more equitable and just society for all.
          Ongoing awareness, education, and advocacy are essential components
          of this transformative journey.

                    In conclusion, as the city marches forward, the journey toward
          gender equality and social inclusion is integral to the nation's identity and
          progress. The commitment to empowering every citizen, irrespective of
          background or identity, reflects not only the aspirations of the people but
          also the nation's resilience and capacity for positive transformation. This
          introduction sets the stage for a comprehensive exploration of the
          initiatives, challenges, and triumphs in the ongoing pursuit of Gender
          Equality and Social Inclusion in the Philippines.

                                  CHAPTER I
                              GENERAL PROVISIONS

                           ARTICLE I - General Principles

       Section 1. Title. This Ordinance shall be known and cited as the “Gender
Equality and Social Inclusion Code of the City of San Fernando, La Union"
hereinafter referred to as the “GESI Code”.

       Section 2. Mandates and Legal Bases. The City Government of San
Fernando, La Union affirms the national and international mandates and
frameworks on gender equality and empowerment and localizes them, pursuant to,
among others, Article 2, Section 14 of the 1987 Philippine Constitution, Republic Act
(RA) 9710 or the Magna Carta of Women, RA 9262 or the Anti-Violence Against
Women and their Children Act of 2004, RA 9208/10364 or the (Expanded) Anti-
Trafficking in Persons Act of 2003/2012, RA 7160 or the Local Government Code of
1991, the General Appropriations Act, and international commitments, particularly
the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women,
the Beijing Platform for Action, and the Sustainable Development Goals.

        Section 3. Declaration of Principles, Policies and Strategies. The City
Government of San Fernando, La Union recognizes that gender discrimination
overlaps with and is compounded by socio-cultural factors such as age, class,
disability, ethnicity, origin, language, religion, health and other status. The City shall
ensure substantive equality in protecting, promoting and fulfilling the human rights
of women and men, and in pursuing their social, economic and political
empowerment. It shall improve the terms for individuals and groups to take part in
society, and the process of improving the ability, opportunities, and dignity of those
disadvantaged on the basis of their identity.

      The City Government shall institute an enabling environment for gender-
responsive governance and shall abolish unequal structures and practices that
perpetuate discrimination and inequality. Towards this end, it shall develop the
capacities of City Government departments and human resource, and promote
equal participation among its constituents in development agenda-setting, policy-
making, planning, programming, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

      Section 4. Definition of Terms. The meaning of terms used in this
Ordinance are adopted from the definitions of the Magna Carta of Women:

       a. “Discrimination” refers to any gender-based distinction, exclusion, or
          restriction which has the effect or purpose of impairing or nullifying the
          recognition, enjoyment, or exercise by women, irrespective of their marital

         status, on a basis of equality of men and women, of human rights and
         fundamental freedoms in the political, economic, social, cultural, civil, or
         any other field.

         It includes any act or omission, including by law, policy, administrative
         measure, or practice, that directly or indirectly excludes or restricts
         women in the recognition and promotion of their rights and their access
         to and enjoyment of opportunities, benefits, or privileges.

         A measure or practice of general application is discrimination against
         women if it fails to provide for mechanisms to offset or address sex or
         gender-based disadvantages or limitations of women, as a result of which
         women are denied or restricted in the recognition and protection of their
         rights and in their access to and enjoyment of opportunities, benefits, or
         privileges; or women, more than men, are shown to have suffered the
         greater adverse effects of those measures or practices.

         Provided, finally, that discrimination compounded by or intersecting with
         other grounds, status, or condition, such as age, ethnicity, origin,
         language, class, disability, health or religion shall be considered
         discrimination against women under this Ordinance.

      b. “Empowerment” refers to the provision, availability, and accessibility of
         opportunities, services, and observance of human rights which enable
         women, the poor, the vulnerable and the disadvantaged to actively
         participate and contribute to the political, economic, social, and cultural
         development of the nation as well as those which shall provide them equal
         access to ownership, management, and control of production, and of
         material and informational resources and benefits in the family,
         community, and society.

      c. “Gender and Development (GAD)” refers to the development
         perspective and process that are participatory and empowering,
         equitable, sustainable, free from violence, respectful of human rights,
         supportive of self-determination and actualization of human potentials. It
         seeks to achieve gender equality as a fundamental value that should be
         reflected in development choices; seeks to transform society’s social,
         economic, and political structures and questions the validity of the gender
         roles they ascribed to women and men; contends that women are active
         agents of development and not just passive recipients of development
         assistance; and stresses the need of women to organize themselves and
         participate in political processes to strengthen their legal rights.

      d. “GAD Budget” refers to the cost of implementing GAD programs which
         shall be at least five percent (5%) of the City’s total budget appropriations.

      e. “Gender Equality” refers to the policies, instruments, programs, services,
         and actions that address the disadvantaged position of women in society
         by providing preferential treatment and affirmative action. Such temporary
         special measures aimed at accelerating de facto equality between men
         and women shall not be considered discriminatory but shall in no way
         entail as a consequence the maintenance of unequal or separate
         standards. These measures shall be discontinued when the objectives of
         equality of opportunity and treatment have been achieved.

      f. “Gender Mainstreaming” refers to the strategy for making women’s as
         well as men’s concerns and experiences an integral dimension of the
         design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of policies and
         programs in all political, economic, and societal spheres so that women
         and men benefit equally and inequality is not perpetuated. It is the process
         of assessing the implications for women and men of any planned action,
         including legislation, policies, or programs in all areas and at all levels.

      g. “Marginalization” refers to a condition where a whole category of people
         is excluded from useful and meaningful participation in political,
         economic, social, and cultural life.

      h. “Marginalized” refers to the basic, disadvantaged, or vulnerable persons
         or groups who are mostly living in poverty and have little or no access to
         land and other resources, basic social and economic services such as
         health care, education, water and sanitation, employment and livelihood
         opportunities, housing, social security, physical infrastructure, and the
         justice system. These include, but are not limited to, women in the
         following sectors and groups:

           (1) “Elderly” or “Senior Citizens” refers to those sixty (60) years of age
               and above;

           (2) “Fisherfolk” refers to those directly or indirectly engaged in taking,
               culturing, or processing fishery or aquatic resources. These include,
               but are not to be limited to, women engaged in fishing in municipal
               waters, coastal and marine areas, women workers in commercial
               fishing and aquaculture, vendors and processors of fish and coastal
               products, and subsistence producers such as shell-gatherers,
               managers, and producers of mangrove resources, and other related
               producers;

           (3) “Migrant Workers” refers to Filipinos who are to be engaged, are
                engaged, or have been engaged in a remunerated activity in a State
                of which they are not legal residents, whether documented or
                undocumented; and

           (4) “Persons with Disabilities (PWD)” or “the disabled” refers to those
               who are suffering from restriction or different abilities, as a result of a
               mental, physical, or sensory impairment to perform an activity in the
               manner or within the range considered normal for a human being.

      i.     “Non-Traditional        Occupation”     refers     to   jobs that    have
           been traditionally filled by one gender, or those in which less than 25% of
           either sex is employed in such occupation.

      j. “Sexual Harassment” refers to any misconduct involving a single act or
         a series of unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or
         other verbal or physical behavior of a sexual nature, made directly or
         indirectly.

           (1) “Sexual Harassment in work, education or training” refers to acts
               committed by an employer, employee, manager, supervisor, agent of
               the employer, teacher, instructor, professor, coach, trainer, or any

              other person who, having authority, influence or moral ascendancy
              over another in work or training or education environment, demands,
              requests or otherwise requires any sexual favor from the other,
              regardless of whether the demand, request or requirement for
              submission is accepted by the object of the said Act; and

           (2) “Sexual Harassment in public spaces” refers to unwanted,
               unwelcome, or uninvited comments, gestures or actions forced on an
               individual in public places without their consent and is directed against
               them because of their sex, gender, gender expression or sexual
               orientation that affect victims not just physically by psychologically
               causing anxiety, fear, depression, post-traumatic stress disorder, or
               other effects damaging their health and well-being.

      k.    “Violence Against Women (VAW)” refers to any act of gender-based
           violence that results in, or is likely to result in, physical, sexual, or
           psychological harm or suffering to women, including threats of such acts,
           coercion, or arbitrary deprivation of liberty, whether occurring in public or
           in private life. It shall be understood to encompass, b(1) Physical, sexual,
           psychological, and economic violence occurring in the family, including
           battering, sexual abuse of female children in the household, dowry-
           related violence, marital rape, and other traditional practices harmful to
           women, non-spousal violence, and violence related to exploitation.

           (1) Physical, sexual, and psychological violence occurring within the
              general community, including rape, sexual abuse, sexual harassment,
              and intimidation at work, in educational institutions and elsewhere,
              trafficking in women, and prostitution; and

           (2) Physical, sexual, and psychological violence perpetrated or condoned
               by the State, wherever it occurs. It also includes acts of violence
               against women as defined in Republic Acts No. 9208 and 9262.

       l. “Workers in the Informal Economy” or “informal workers” refers to self-
          employed, occasionally or personally hired, or sub-contracted workers; or
          paid or unpaid family workers in household incorporated or unincorporated
          enterprises; including home workers, micro-entrepreneurs and producers,
          and operators of sari-sari-stores and all other categories who are
          vulnerable to violations of workers’ rights.

                           CHAPTER II
      GENDER EQUALITY AND SOCIAL INCLUSION IN DEVELOPMENT

                 ARTICLE II – Gender-Responsive Governance

      Section 5. Adoption of the Gender Mainstreaming Strategy. The City
Government of San Fernando, La Union shall adopt gender mainstreaming as a
strategy in the pursuit of gender-responsive governance wherein gender equality
and social inclusion goals are integrated into the development plans, programs and
services of the city government, and in the whole planning and budgeting cycle,
including, but not limited to the following: Comprehensive Development Plan, City
Development Investment Program, Annual Investment Program, Executive and
Legislative Agenda, Capacity Development Plan, Sustainable Agriculture and
Fishery Program, Solid Waste Management Plan, Disaster Risk Management and

Preparedness Plan, Climate Change Adaptation Plan, City Health Plan, City Social
Welfare Plan, Comprehensive Land Use Plan, Zoning Ordinance, Socio-Economic
Development Plan and Multi-Year Investment Program.

      Section 6. Gender-Responsive Human Resource. The responsibility for
gender mainstreaming rests on every official and personnel of the City Government.
To ensure gender-responsiveness of the City Government human resource, the
Human Resource and Management Office shall include gender and development in
performance targets, key result areas, and career and personnel development plan
of City Government human resource, and conduct continued capacity development
programs on the gender sensitivity, gender-related laws and policies, gender-
responsive planning and budgeting for employees and officials, including the
Sangguniang Panlungsod and Sangguniang Barangay Members.

     The Gender Focal Point System Technical Working Group (GFPS TWG) shall
develop and maintain an inventory of capacity development activities on gender and
development completed by employees and officials, including members of decision-
making bodies within the City Government.

     Section 7. Adoption of Gender-Fair Language. The City Government of San
Fernando, La Union shall adopt the use of gender-neutral language in all its official
documents, communications and issuances. This shall cover all government
agencies and units, local and state universities and colleges, and government-
owned and controlled corporations within the territorial jurisdiction of the City.

      Section 8. Information Dissemination on Gender Equality and Social
Inclusion. The City Government of San Fernando, La Union shall develop
information, education and communication campaign materials to increase
knowledge among constituents on their equal human rights and the City’s programs,
projects, activities and services, thus encourage their participation and engagement
with the City Government.

      Section 9. GESI Website. The City Government of San Fernando, La Union
shall establish a GESI website where the City’s gender equality and social inclusion
information and initiatives can be accessed and obtained by its constituents.

      Section 10. Women Representatives to the City Development Council
(CDC) and Barangay Development Council (BDC). The City Government of San
Fernando, La Union shall ensure that at least one third (1/3) of the members of the
CDC and the BDC are women. It shall ensure women’s representation and
participation in barangay and community-based conflict resolution structures (i.e.,
Lupong Tagapamayapa) and local peace and order councils tasked to recommend
policies and programs on peace issues in the community.

     In cooperation with the Department of the Interior and Local Government -
Region I, the City Government shall conduct capacity development activities to
increase the knowledge and skills on gender-responsiveness among members of
the CDC, BDC and other decision-making bodies within the City Government.

        Section 11. Women in Organizations. The City Government of San
Fernando, La Union shall encourage and support the organization of women at the
barangay level, and women with special needs or those in especially difficult
circumstances (e.g., differently-abled, elderly, etc.).  It shall promote their
participation in the City’s decision-making processes and development activities.

The City Government shall likewise encourage efforts of mixed organizations to
increase women’s membership and leadership.

      The City Social Welfare and Development Office (CSWDO) shall collect and
maintain a database on the City’s women’s organizations, and mixed organizations’
disaggregated data of women and men members and in leadership positions.

      Section 12. Support to Women with Special Concerns. The City
Government of San Fernando, La Union shall promote the human rights to
development of women with special concerns.

        a. People with Disabilities (PWD). The City Government shall initiate active
advocacy on the rights of people with disabilities, especially women. It shall ensure

and toilet facilities for PWDs. The City Government shall implement Republic Act
7277 or the Magna Carta for Disabled Persons that reserves five percent (5%) of
contractual or emergency positions for persons with disabilities.

       b. Elderly Women. The Barangay GAD Focal Point shall organize elderly
women within the barangay to promote their welfare. The City Government shall
pay attention to the differential needs of women and men in the implementation of
programs, such as UMISU, that provide emergency assistance and support
appropriate socio-economic activities for the elderly.

      c. Women in Detention. The City Government shall protect the rights of
women in detention through appropriate programs to respond to their specific
needs, including those related to reproductive health. It shall provide separate
physical structures or spaces for women and men’s detention and rehabilitation.

                               ARTICLE III – Health

        Section 13. Comprehensive Gender-Responsive Health Care Service
Delivery. The City Government of San Fernando, La Union shall ensure that
delivery of health services does not discriminate on account of sexual orientation,
gender identity and expression, age, class, disability, ethnicity, origin, language,
religion, health and other status. The City shall promote and fulfill women’s rights
over their own bodies, and through the City health services, shall support their
decision on the number and spacing of children. The City Health Office (CHO) shall
involve women and girls in planning health programs and in decision-making, and
develop health programs that encourage constituents to access and demand quality
and respectful services for women and girls.

       Section 14. Reproductive Health Services for All. All public hospitals in
the City of San Fernando, La Union shall offer equitable and affordable reproductive
health services for all regardless of sex, age and social status. The CHO shall
promote male engagement in reproductive health programs.

      Section 15. Gender-Responsive Health Human Resource Development.
The City Health Office, in coordination with the Department of Health (DOH), shall
formulate a health human resource development plan that will ensure the sufficient
number of skilled health professionals to attend to all deliveries; and a human
resource deployment program to ensure the availability of qualified health service
providers to meet the City’s needs.

       In coordination with the DOH and the academe, the City Health Office shall
enjoin community-based health professionals to undertake training on gender-
sensitive, gender-based violence (GBV)-responsive and respectful health service
delivery. It shall develop capacities of the local health board to be sensitive and
responsive to the health needs of girls, female adolescents, women senior citizens,
and those in especially difficult circumstances.

       Section 16. Community Dialogues. The City Health Office, in coordination
with barangay officials, shall organize continuing dialogues with women and men in
the communities to increase knowledge on social and cultural barriers to health
(such as stigma attached to HIV/AIDS and mental health issues, risks of teenage
pregnancy, etc.) and how to address them.

       Section 17. Gender-Sensitive Pre-Marriage and Family Planning
Counseling. The CHO shall integrate the gender perspective in its pre-marriage
and family planning counseling and education programs. It shall conduct gender
sensitivity trainings and gender issues in health among counselors and educators.

      Pre-marriage counseling and family planning education and counseling
programs conducted by the City shall integrate the gender perspective in modules
and materials. Likewise, educators and counselors shall also undergo trainings on
gender sensitivity and on women’s sexuality and health concerns.

                                ARTICLE IV – Education

        SECTION 18. Education for All. The City Government of San Fernando,
La Union shall ensure that education is available, accessible, affordable, non-
discriminatory and fair to all women and men, regardless of sexual orientation,
gender identity and expression, age, class, disability, ethnicity, origin, language,
religion, health and other status.

       All women and men constituents of the City desiring to engage themselves
in non-formal, functional and practical education shall enlist with the CSWDO. The
CSWDO shall coordinate with the Department of Education for the conduct of
weekend literacy classes in identified schools in the barangays to accommodate
learners who have work during the weekdays.

       SECTION 19. Capacity Development on GAD for Personnel of Academic
Institutions. The City Government of San Fernando, La Union shall encourage
public and private educational institutions to conduct capacity development on GAD
among its teaching and non-teaching personnel and management.

       The Gender Focal Point System Technical Working Group (GFPS TWG)
shall develop and maintain an inventory of capacity development activities on
gender and development conducted for personnel of educational institutions in the
City.

       SECTION 20. Monitoring Gender Stereotypes in Educational Materials.
The City’s GFPS TWG shall work closely with the Government Retirees Association
of San Fernando City, La Union (GRASFLU) or association of retired teachers if
there will be organized, to regularly review education and instructional materials
from a gender perspective, and report to the Office of the Mayor and the City Division
of Schools any stereotype or discrimination against any group of people found
therein.

       Section 21. Gender-Sensitive Career Counselling. The City Youth
Development Office (CYDO), in coordination with public and private high schools in
the City of San Fernando, La Union, shall organize gender-sensitive career
counseling activities to encourage students to pursue non-traditional academic,
technical and vocational courses that widen their career choices.

        Section 22. Equitable Scholarship Program. The City of San Fernando,
La Union, shall give equitable scholarship opportunities to female and male
students. The CYDO, in coordination with the Office of the City Mayor and the GFPS
TWG, shall design a scholarship program that encourages students to take non-
traditional courses, and implement the same in cooperation with senior high schools,
and tertiary, technical and vocational educational institutions in the City.

       The CSWDO shall recommend survivors of violence against women (VAWC)
and/or their children for scholarship and study grants.

       Section 23. Scholarships for City Government Employees. In no case
shall gender, age and disability be the basis for disqualification from availing
scholarship grants or learning and development opportunities. As such, pregnant
women, women with child/children, solo parents, senior employees and differently-
abled employees shall be considered for nomination and selection in scholarship
grants provided that they apply and/or transfer their learning within the prescribed
period for return service.

                          ARTICLE V – Labor and Employment

       Section 24. Equal Access to Employment, Training and Promotion. No
woman or man shall be denied of employment opportunity on account of sexual
orientation, gender identity or expression, age, language, ethnicity, creed, religion
and civil status as prescribed in the Labor Code, as amended by RA 6725. Likewise,
no one shall be denied of training and promotion in employment on account of
aforementioned factors. Violations shall be penalized in accordance with the
provisions of the Labor Code and its Implementing Rules and Regulations and other
existing national laws protecting women.

        Women and men shall be given equal opportunity to acquire training on non-
traditional occupation.

       Section 25. Wage and Benefits. Every employer shall comply with the
minimum wage as prescribed by the Regional Wage Board and shall grant all
benefits to all employees such as maternity/paternity leave, sick and vacation leave,
retirement, termination and other benefits provided by law.

       Section 26. Facilities and Support Systems for Workers. All offices,
agencies and establishments or companies located in the City of San Fernando,
public or private, shall provide services that support balancing family obligations and
work responsibilities, such as but not limited to the following: day care and child-
minding centers, breastfeeding or lactation stations with appropriate facilities and
corresponding nursing/lactation breaks, and flexible working hours, among others.

        The City Government shall establish and maintain a child-minding facility for
the children of its employees. This could help the employees focus on their work
resulting to an improved work performance. In consideration of the financial
limitation of the City Government, the parent-employees shall be responsible for the
payment of the worker’s salary to ensure its continued operation. The maintenance

and operating expenses of the facility, including electricity, water, furniture and
fixture shall be borne by the City Government.

       Section 27. Prevention and Response to Sexual Harassment in the
Workplace. All offices, agencies and establishments or companies located in the
City of San Fernando, public or private, shall prevent and address cases of sexual
harassment in the workplace.

       a. A committee on decorum and investigation shall be set up in all offices,
          agencies, and establishments or companies to prevent, resolve and
          redress sexual harassment cases.

       b. All offices, agencies, and establishments or companies in the City of San
          Fernando, public or private, shall conduct orientation on sexual
          harassment and Republic Act 7877.

       c. The City’s Local Economic and Business Development Office (LEBDO)
          shall create and maintain a database to track compliance of offices,
          agencies and establishments or companies of this provision of the Code.

       d. Failure to comply with this provision shall be penalized according to
          those imposed by RA 7877 and other relevant laws.

       Section 28. Protection of Household-based Workers or Women in the
Informal Economy. The City Government of San Fernando, La Union shall ensure
that social protection is accorded to women working in the informal economy. These
are vendors, sewers, laundry workers, house helpers and other similar occupations.

      The City Government shall require households to enlist their female and male
house helpers with their respective Barangays. The CSWDO shall establish and
maintain a database of house helpers from the list submitted by the Barangays to
ensure fair working arrangements such as reasonable work hours, regular days off
and payment of employer-employee contributions to the Social Security System
(SSS) and Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and to monitor
cases of sexual harassment, sexual abuse and other forms of maltreatment.

     The CSWDO shall conduct a registration of women involved in the informal
economy for consultation and provision of appropriate support services.

      Section 29. Rights of Women in the Entertainment Industry. Women
engaged in legitimate occupations in the entertainment industry, such as singers,
stage performers or actresses, baristas and receptionists, shall be recognized as
wage earners, are entitled to at least a minimum wage and benefits afforded to
women workers, and shall render services as set in the job contract and only in the
place of work as specified in the business permit of the establishment concerned.
Employing minors is prohibited in accordance with Republic Act 7658 or An Act
Prohibiting the Employment of Children below 15 Years of Age in Public and Private
Undertakings.

         ARTICLE VI – Agriculture, Fisheries and Natural Resources

      Section 30. Rural Women’s Participation. The City of San Fernando, La
Union recognizes women as farmers and fisher folk, and shall give them equal
opportunities to directly, actively and fully participate in policy and program


City Ord. No. 2025-08, page 11 of 27 pages

formulation, planning and design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of
agricultural, coastal and natural resource management programs and projects.

       The City Agriculture Office (CAO) shall ensure participation of rural women
by tapping existing peoples’ organizations, NGOs and rural women groups in the
implementation of programs on sustainable agriculture and fisheries such as but not
limited to production of organic inputs and fertilizers, natural farming and vermin
composting, and other technologies.

       Section 31. Sex-Disaggregated Database of Farmers and Fisherfolks.
The CAO shall continue the registration of fisherfolk, fishing vessels and gears as
stated in Section 19 of the Philippine Fisheries Code of 1998, and shall maintain
and update the City’s sex-disaggregated database of farmers and fisherfolk.

      Section 32. Women’s Representation in Resource Management
Council. The City Agriculture Office in coordination with the City Environment and
Natural Resources Office shall designate coastal areas to be managed by women.

        Each barangay shall create and support a resource management council
which shall ensure promotion and protection of ecological balance and full
participation of women.

       Section 33. Gender-Responsive Food Security. The CAO shall design and
undertake community-based gender-responsive programs and projects on food
security, including organic farming, community seed banking and farmer-to-farmer
seed exchange.

                     ARTICLE VII – Industry, Trade and Tourism

         Section 34. Inclusive Economic Empowerment. The City Government of
San Fernando, La Union shall pursue the economic empowerment of women and
the marginalized. It shall design and implement a gender-responsive livelihood and
microenterprise program involving skills and leadership training, business start-up
and management assistance, technology support, common service/ production
facilities, product development, market access, and other business development
services.

       Section 35. Sex-Disaggregated Database of Entrepreneurs. The City
Government of San Fernando, La Union, shall require informal businesses to enlist
with their respective Barangays. The Local Economic and Business Development
Office (LEBDO) shall establish and maintain a sex-disaggregated database of
registered businesses in the City and those submitted by the barangays, as well as
cooperatives and organizations engaged in the production, distribution, and
exchange of goods and services.

       Section 36. Capital Assistance for Women. The City of San Fernando, La
Union shall enhance access of women to capital assistance. In consultation and
coordination with all local departments and offices engaged in lending programs,
LEBDO, the City Agriculture Office and the City Social Welfare and Development
Office shall open a lending window to provide capital assistance to women
entrepreneurs or organizations undertaking enterprise development and market
expansion.

       Section 37. Gender-Responsive Tourism Development. In consultation

       Section 56. Participation in GAD-Related Inter-Agency Mechanisms.
The GAD Office shall ensure the active participation of the City Government in inter-
agency mechanisms related to gender and development, such as but not limited to
the Inter-Agency Council against Trafficking, City Council for the Protection of
Children, Juvenile Justice Welfare Agency and Region 1 Advocates for Gender
Equality (RAGE 1).

       As an active member of the Provincial Inter-Agency Council Against
Trafficking, the City Government shall contribute to the formulation and
implementation of a comprehensive program to suppress the trafficking of women
and children; conduct of massive information campaigns; formulation and
implementation of a program for returning victims in cooperation with local offices of
DOLE, TESDA and other government agencies involved in training and livelihood
development. The City’s GAD TWG shall ensure that women going abroad as
fiancées of foreign nationals undertake guidance and counseling and refer traffic
victims to appropriate agencies.

       Section 57. Technical Assistance on GAD to Barangays. The City
Government of San Fernando, La Union, in cooperation with the CLGOO, shall
provide technical assistance to every barangay in identifying and addressing gender
and development issues. The City’s GAD Office, in partnership with the City
Planning Office, shall organize gender-responsive barangay development strategic
planning workshops and other activities to guide GAD planning, budgeting and
reporting of barangays, including the use of the Gender-Responsive Local
Governance (GeRL) Tool.

      The GAD Office shall maintain a database on the performance of barangays
on gender mainstreaming, such as the formal appointment of gender focal points,

GAD plans and budgets, capacity development on GAD among barangay officials

        Section 58. Monitoring, Evaluation and Documentation of GAD
Initiatives. The GAD Office shall identify gender and development indicators to
measure results of its gender equality and social inclusion initiatives. It shall
establish baseline data and maintain sex-disaggregated data, in coordination with
sectoral departments and barangays.

       The GAD Office shall develop tools/ reporting templates and establish
mechanisms for effective monitoring and evaluation of progress and results of
gender equality and social inclusion initiatives, including a system of rewards and
incentives.

       The GAD Office shall establish a system of documenting the activities, best
practices and lessons learned in line with strengthening the efforts on promoting
gender equality and social inclusion in the City.

       Section 59. Budget. For the effective implementation of this GESI Code,
the City Government of San Fernando shall appropriate and spend at least five
percent (5%) of the City’s annual budget to implement its GAD Agenda and annual
GAD Plan and Budget, pursuant to RA 9710.

      Section 60. Compliance Report. Within one (1) year from the effectivity of
this Code and every year thereafter, all Local Government departments and
barangays, shall submit a report to the Sangguniang Panlungsod on their
compliance with this Code.
                                  CHAPTER IV
                               FINAL PROVISIONS

       Section 61. Independent Action for Damages. The filing of a complaint
under this Code shall not preclude the offended party from pursuing other remedies
available under the law and to invoke any of the provisions of existing laws,
especially those recently enacted laws protecting women and children, including the
Women in Development and Nation Building Act (Republic Act No. 7192), the
Special Protection of Children Against Child Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination
Act (Republic Act No. 7610), the Anti-Sexual Harassment Act of 1995 (Republic Act
No. 7877), the Anti-Rape Law of 1997 (Republic Act No. 8353), the Rape Victim
Assistance and Protection Act of 1998 (Republic Act No. 8505), the Anti-Trafficking
in Persons Act of 2003 (Republic Act No. 9208), and the Anti-Violence Against
Women and Their Children Act of 2004 (Republic Act No. 9262). If violence has
been proven to be perpetrated by agents of the State including, but not limited to,
extrajudicial killings, enforced disappearances, torture, and internal displacements,
such shall be considered aggravating offenses with corresponding penalties
depending on the severity of the offenses.

       Section 62. Repealing Clause. All Ordinances, issuances, rules and
regulations inconsistent with the provisions of this Ordinance are hereby repealed,
amended, rescinded and/or modified accordingly.
